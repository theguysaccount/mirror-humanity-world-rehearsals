# Product: Shift Rehearsal

**User.** People who write the rota for a small service business: café and shop owners, clinic and front-desk managers, event organisers. They decide staffing every week with gut feel or a spreadsheet.

**Painful job.** "Do I need a third person on Saturday morning?" Too few staff means long lines and walk-outs; too many means paying people to stand around. Spreadsheets average the day and miss the rush; queueing calculators assume a steady hour and nobody leaving.

**Promise.** In two minutes, see what a staffing change does to waits, walk-outs, idle hours and cost across hundreds of simulated days, with every assumption written down and a link to share with a partner or manager.

**Wedge.** Searches like "how many staff do I need", "queue wait time calculator", "how many baristas for 50 customers an hour"; the staffing table page answers them directly. Scenario links travel between owners, managers and accountants.

**Why now.** Labour is most small operators' biggest cost; POS systems now export customers per hour; a browser can run hundreds of simulated days instantly, so no server or account is needed.

**Activation metric.** A visitor changes an input from the example and copies a scenario link or downloads the summary (`copy_link`, `summary`), once analytics are on.

**First revenue event.** A business pays for saved, scored studies on other hosting: scenarios kept over time with a running accuracy score from reality checks. The slot exists (`STUDIES_URL`).

**What v1 deliberately won't do.**
- Forecast demand, or claim results predict a real day: everything is labelled a simulation.
- Synthetic populations, AI agents or "simulate humanity" claims: nothing here has been validated against real populations, so the tool doesn't claim it.
- Accounts, servers or uploads: scenarios live in the link, reality checks in the browser.
- Multi-line, priority or multi-skill queues, breaks and shift-length rules (later, if reality checks show they matter).
